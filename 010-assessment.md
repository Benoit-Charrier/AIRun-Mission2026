## Knowledge Check

**Q1. What is the goal maturity level of this program, and what are the three parts of the self-study course, in order?**

The goal maturity level is Level 3 (AI-native / Frontier). The three parts of the self-study course, in order, are: Foundation (modules 010, 020, 030), the 10 role modules (100–1000), and the Assembly Line (module 1111).

---

**Q2. A role module's Theory has two parts and its Practice has three kata types. Name them, and say who does each and what the Final Kata produces.**

Theory has two parts:
- **Wide Theory** — all learners; shared literacy to read, evaluate, and hand off the role's artefacts.
- **Deep Theory** — your one chosen role; specialist depth on advanced moves, anti-patterns, and human-owned decisions.

Practice has three kata types:
- **Wide Kata** — all learners; 15-minute exercise, output readable outside the role.
- **Deep Kata** — your one role; 30-minute specialist artefact pack for real delivery.
- **Final Kata** — all learners; ~60 minutes; produces a runnable spec for the role's Agent or Skill. A non-specialist produces a thinner version; a specialist a production-grade one. The ten specs chain into the AI Factory in Module 1111.

---

**Q3. What is a carry-forward artefact, and why does the course require one running case across all modules rather than a fresh example per module?**

A carry-forward artefact is the one reusable thing a module produces that a later module consumes as input. The course requires one running case because the artefacts chain: each module's output is the next module's input. Switching scenarios mid-course breaks that chain and produces 10 disconnected exercises instead of one coherent delivery system.

## Self-Reflections

**Reflection 1. Take one task you finished in the last week the old way. Map it onto the L3 delivery loop. What concretely changes, and which single habit keeps you on the old way?**

Task: writing weekly feedback on the FDE program, then asking AI to summarise it.

I was already running a partial version of the L3 loop — I produced the raw observations (the spec input) and the agent drafted the structured summary. What concretely changes under full L3 is that the edited summary file becomes the source of truth rather than living in the chat. The spec would explicitly state: "Here are my five raw observations — draft structured feedback grouped by theme, with one concrete suggestion per theme." My edits on top of the draft are the deliverable; the file, not the conversation, is the memory.

The single habit that keeps me on the old way: the belief that doing it myself is faster, better quality, more genuine, and more of a learning opportunity. Two of those are true at L1. At L3 the genuine part is preserved — my signal and judgment stay human-owned; the agent handles structure and language.

**Reflection 2. Name the one adjacent role whose Wide module you are most tempted to skip. What artefact passes between that role and yours, and what would you be unable to evaluate or hand off cleanly if you never built that literacy?**

The Engineering role is the one I am most tempted to skip — it is the most technically distant from my day-to-day work.

The artefact that passes between Engineering and Delivery/PM is the **spec**: the context files, agent definitions, and task specs that engineers work from. Without Engineering Wide literacy, I can set a delivery target but I cannot judge whether the spec my team is working from is production-grade or dangerously thin. That gap would show up at handoff and at checkpoints — I would be approving work I cannot actually evaluate.
