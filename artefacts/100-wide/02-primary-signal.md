**Context from K1.W.1–K1.W.2**

1. Loyalty fragmentation → 6–8% churn

3. Competitive moat erosion (Zalando/ASOS/About You ahead on unified loyalty)

## Competitor Teardown: Zalando Plus (Loyalty Flow)

### Solved by Zalando Plus for EU Fashion Segment
- **Unified EU account & loyalty:** One login, one loyalty balance across all 15 markets. Verbatim test (walkthrough, May 2026): Created account in Germany, logged in from Italy, Austria, France — same balance, same tier status across all.
- **Real-time points sync:** Points posted within hours of purchase. No regional silos. Any EU purchase adds to the same pool.
- **Subscription + gamification:** €5.99/month removes friction; tier acceleration (Silver → Gold within 6 months if subscriber) creates retention lock-in.
- **Frictionless checkout:** Mobile-first: scan Zalando Plus card or barcode, payment completes in <10 seconds. No payment method re-entry per country.
- **Local payment methods + SCA compliance:** Supports 14 local payment methods (Postepay IT, IDEAL NL, Przelewy24 PL, etc.) with SCA built in. No transaction failures from regional payment incompatibility.

### Partially Solved
- **In-store integration:** Zalando Plus is online-first. In-store presence is a partner network (not owned stores), so loyalty sync in partner locations is inconsistent. For Meridian's owned-store footprint, this is a vulnerability.
- **Personalization at scale:** Zalando's ML personalization is strong but lags AI-native competitors (no agentic recommendations yet; rule-based tier acceleration).

### Unsolved / Gaps
- **Local language + cultural UX:** Zalando's core UX is German-first; Italian, Polish, and Spanish experiences feel translated rather than native. Opportunity: Meridian's regional teams could deliver more localized UX per country within a unified loyalty engine.
- **Cross-segment loyalty (e.g., fashion + home + electronics):** Zalando only does fashion. Meridian's multi-category footprint (home, electronics, fashion) means opportunity for cross-category loyalty acceleration (e.g., buy 3 home items, get 2x fashion points). Zalando can't do this.
- **Omnichannel inventory visibility in loyalty:** Zalando Plus doesn't show real-time store inventory in the app or loyalty journey. Meridian's 1,400-store footprint is an advantage if loyalty UX surface this inventory (e.g., "Check stock in nearby stores, get 50 bonus points if you collect in-store instead of shipping").

**Teardown verdict:** Zalando Plus is the model to match. Meridian's owned-store advantage is a moat *if* loyalty integrates store inventory and local language depth; otherwise, Zalando's simplicity wins.

## Re-Rating K1.W.2 Pain Points Against Primary Signal

### Pain Point 1: Loyalty Fragmentation → Customer Churn
**Status: ✅ CONFIRMED + SHARPENED**

**Primary signal evidence:**
- Verbatims A, B, C, F show explicit churn to Zalando/ASOS due to fragmentation.
- Support ticket aggregate (C) shows ~12 cross-border frustration tickets/month.
- Customer survey (H) ranks points fragmentation #1 churn driver (23%).

**Sharpening:** The pain is not abstract; it's explicit cross-border travel scenarios (A, B) and the moment of failed sync at checkout (C). The switch to Zalando happens *because* Zalando's loyalty worked where Meridian's didn't. This moves from "customers want unified loyalty" to "customers are actively leaving when they try to use loyalty across borders."

**Confidence level:** High. Backed by Trustpilot, support tickets, in-app reviews, and internal survey.

---

### Pain Point 2: Regulatory Compliance Drift
**Status: ⚠️ SHARPENED (but lower in primary signal than expected)**

**Primary signal evidence:**
- Verbatim H (customer survey) does not list regulatory compliance as a stated pain (only UX/account issues).
- Competitor teardown (Zalando) shows payment method + SCA compliance is already solved by mature players; not a customer-facing friction in 2026.
- However, teardown reveals that local payment method support (Postepay IT, Przelewy24 PL) is table stakes, not a differentiator.

**Sharpening:** Regulatory compliance is real (EDPB, PSD2, VAT rules from K1.W.2) but is an *operational/legal binding constraint*, not a *customer-facing pain point*. Customers don't complain about VAT; they complain about loyalty not working. Compliance is a prerequisite for launching, not a use-case justification.

**Confidence level:** Medium. Regulatory risk is material but not the driver of churn in primary signal.

---

### Pain Point 3: Competitive Moat Erosion
**Status: ✅ CONFIRMED (with calendar urgency)**

**Primary signal evidence:**
- Verbatim F: Zalando Plus €5.99/mo tier model explicitly named as the switching driver.
- Verbatim G: Market perception that unification is table stakes; Meridian is "stuck in 2020," competitors "figured this out 2 years ago."
- Verbatim H: 18% of churn respondents cite "ASOS/Zalando have better loyalty" explicitly.
- Competitor teardown: Zalando Plus is mature, scaled, and actively recruiting MRG's repeat customers.

**Sharpening:** The moat erosion is not future-risk; it's happening now. Verbatim F (May 2026) shows the exact moment Zalando Plus triggered a switch. The market is no longer waiting for MRG to catch up; competitors are actively recruiting *now*. The window to defend this segment is 12–18 months, after which repeat customers will have already migrated to Zalando/ASOS/About You's loyalty ecosystems (high switching cost once you've optimized your spending patterns for another loyalty engine).

**Confidence level:** High. Backed by Trustpilot, Reddit, and explicit customer interviews.
